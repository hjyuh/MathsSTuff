# Block A — Honors Geometry (remaining units under the current assumption)

## Day A1 — Quadrilateral classification on the coordinate plane

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 4: G.GPE.6, G.CO.10, G.GPE.8

**Mini-lesson.** Start Block A with coordinate classification. Use slope, distance, and midpoint to tell rectangles, rhombi, squares, parallelograms, kites, and trapezoids apart. The main exam risk here is choosing the most specific name, not just a true name.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Use slope to test parallel and perpendicular sides.
- Use distance to test congruent sides and diagonals.
- A square must satisfy both rectangle and rhombus conditions.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Classify the quadrilateral with vertices A(0,0), B(4,0), C(4,3), D(0,3).
   **Solution.** AB and CD are horizontal, so they are parallel. BC and AD are vertical, so they are parallel and also perpendicular to AB. Opposite sides have equal lengths: AB=4, BC=3, CD=4, AD=3. The figure is a rectangle. It is not a square because adjacent side lengths are not equal.
2. **Problem.** Find the midpoint of diagonal AC for A(1,2) and C(7,8).
   **Solution.** Use the midpoint formula: ((1+7)/2, (2+8)/2)=(4,5).
3. **Problem.** Find the distance between P(-2,1) and Q(4,5).
   **Solution.** Distance = √((4-(-2))^2+(5-1)^2)=√(6^2+4^2)=√52=2√13.
4. **Problem.** A quadrilateral has exactly one pair of parallel sides. Name the most specific classification from the course list.
   **Solution.** A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides is a trapezoid.
5. **Problem.** Explain why a square is also a rectangle and a rhombus.
   **Solution.** A rectangle is a parallelogram with four right angles; a rhombus is a parallelogram with four congruent sides. A square has both four right angles and four congruent sides, so it satisfies both definitions.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. What slopes prove two lines are perpendicular?
2. What formula finds a midpoint?
3. A rectangle with all sides congruent is what figure?

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. negative reciprocals; 2. ((x1+x2)/2,(y1+y2)/2); 3. square

## Day A2 — Parallelogram properties and proofs

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 4: G.CO.10, G.SRT.6, G.CO.4

**Mini-lesson.** Build formal proofs for parallelograms. Finals often ask you to move both directions: prove a figure is a parallelogram from conditions, and prove consequences once you know it is one.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Opposite sides and opposite angles of a parallelogram are congruent.
- Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
- A rectangle is a parallelogram with congruent diagonals.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** State two properties of a parallelogram.
   **Solution.** Opposite sides are congruent, and diagonals bisect each other. Opposite angles are also congruent.
2. **Problem.** In parallelogram ABCD, if AB=12 and BC=7, find CD and AD.
   **Solution.** Opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent, so CD=12 and AD=7.
3. **Problem.** In parallelogram WXYZ, diagonals intersect at M. If WM=9 and MY=9, what theorem is being used?
   **Solution.** The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so M is the midpoint of each diagonal.
4. **Problem.** If a quadrilateral has both pairs of opposite sides congruent, what can you conclude?
   **Solution.** That condition is a converse parallelogram test, so the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
5. **Problem.** A parallelogram has congruent diagonals. What special parallelogram must it be?
   **Solution.** A parallelogram with congruent diagonals is a rectangle.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Opposite angles in a parallelogram are _____.
2. Diagonals of a parallelogram _____ each other.
3. A parallelogram with congruent diagonals is a _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. congruent; 2. bisect; 3. rectangle

## Day A3 — Special quadrilaterals, perimeter, and proof strategy

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 4: G.GPE.6, G.GPE.8, G.CO.10

**Mini-lesson.** Refine the family tree: parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus, square, kite, trapezoid. Pair classification with perimeter from coordinates so the algebra and geometry stay linked.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Always ask what extra property upgrades the classification.
- Perimeter from coordinates comes from repeated distance formula use.
- A valid proof needs definitions plus previously established theorems.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Find the perimeter of a square with vertices (0,0), (2,2), (4,0), (2,-2).
   **Solution.** One side length is distance between (0,0) and (2,2): √8=2√2. Perimeter is 4(2√2)=8√2.
2. **Problem.** Name the most specific quadrilateral with four congruent sides but no right-angle information.
   **Solution.** Rhombus.
3. **Problem.** Name the most specific quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel and congruent.
   **Solution.** That is enough to prove the figure is a parallelogram.
4. **Problem.** A quadrilateral has perpendicular diagonals and exactly two pairs of adjacent congruent sides. Classify it.
   **Solution.** Kite.
5. **Problem.** Why is every square a parallelogram?
   **Solution.** A square has two pairs of opposite sides parallel, which is the defining property of a parallelogram.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. A rhombus with four right angles is a _____.
2. A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides is a _____.
3. Perimeter is found by adding all _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. square; 2. parallelogram; 3. side lengths

## Day A4 — Dilations and the meaning of similarity

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 5: G.SRT.1, G.SRT.1a, G.SRT.1b, G.SRT.2, G.SRT.4

**Mini-lesson.** Shift from congruence to similarity. Dilations preserve angle measure and multiply lengths by a scale factor, so they are the engine behind similar figures.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- A dilation sends lines not through the center to parallel lines.
- Side lengths scale by the scale factor; angle measures do not change.
- Similar figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** A dilation has center O and scale factor 3. What happens to a segment of length 5?
   **Solution.** Its image has length 15 because all distances from the center are multiplied by 3.
2. **Problem.** Do angle measures change under a dilation?
   **Solution.** No. Dilations preserve angle measure.
3. **Problem.** If triangle A'B'C' is a dilation of triangle ABC with scale factor 1/2, how do corresponding side lengths compare?
   **Solution.** Each image side is half the corresponding preimage side.
4. **Problem.** Why do lines not passing through the center of dilation map to parallel lines?
   **Solution.** That is a core dilation property: every point moves along rays from the center, preserving direction and producing a parallel image line.
5. **Problem.** If two figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides, what are they?
   **Solution.** Similar figures.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. A dilation preserves angle _____.
2. A scale factor greater than 1 causes an _____.
3. Similar figures have proportional corresponding _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. measure; 2. enlargement; 3. sides

## Day A5 — Similarity transformations and triangle similarity criteria

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 5: G.SRT.3, G.SRT.4, G.SRT.6

**Mini-lesson.** Practice describing sequences of transformations and using AA, SAS~, and SSS~. On a final, you may need to explain why similarity holds, not just state it.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- AA is enough to prove triangle similarity.
- SAS~ needs the included angle equal and surrounding sides proportional.
- Once triangles are similar, all matching side ratios are equal.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Triangles have angle pairs 40°,60°,80° and 40°,60°,80°. Are they similar? Why?
   **Solution.** Yes. By AA similarity, matching two angles is enough, and the third angle matches automatically.
2. **Problem.** Two triangles have included angle equal and surrounding sides in ratio 2:3 and 4:6. Are they similar?
   **Solution.** Yes. The side ratios are equal because 2/3=4/6, and the included angle is equal, so SAS similarity applies.
3. **Problem.** If ΔABC ~ ΔDEF and AB=6 corresponds to DE=9, what is the scale factor from ABC to DEF?
   **Solution.** Scale factor = 9/6 = 3/2.
4. **Problem.** If ΔABC ~ ΔDEF and AC=10 corresponds to DF, with scale factor 3/2, find DF.
   **Solution.** DF = (3/2)(10)=15.
5. **Problem.** Why is AAA usually named AA in school geometry?
   **Solution.** Because once two pairs of angles match, the third pair must also match by the angle-sum theorem.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. AA proves triangle _____.
2. Similar triangles have equal corresponding _____.
3. A scale factor of 2 doubles all corresponding _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. similarity; 2. angles; 3. lengths

## Day A6 — Triangle proportionality and the Pythagorean Theorem from similarity

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 5: G.SRT.5, G.SRT.6

**Mini-lesson.** Use a line parallel to one side of a triangle to create proportional segments. Then connect similarity to the classic proof of the Pythagorean Theorem.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally.
- The converse also matters on finals.
- Similarity can turn geometric pictures into algebraic equations.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. If AD=3, DB=2, and AE=6, find EC.
   **Solution.** A line parallel to one side divides the other two sides proportionally, so AD/DB = AE/EC. Thus 3/2 = 6/EC, giving EC=4.
2. **Problem.** State the converse of the triangle proportionality theorem.
   **Solution.** If a line cuts two sides of a triangle proportionally, then it is parallel to the third side.
3. **Problem.** What is the Pythagorean Theorem?
   **Solution.** In a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c, a^2+b^2=c^2.
4. **Problem.** A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.
   **Solution.** c=√(25+144)=√169=13.
5. **Problem.** Why is similarity useful in proving the Pythagorean Theorem?
   **Solution.** Dropping an altitude in a right triangle creates smaller triangles similar to the original, letting side products turn into the equation a^2+b^2=c^2.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. If a segment is parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides _____.
2. In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the _____.
3. The converse proportionality theorem proves a line is _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. proportionally; 2. legs; 3. parallel

## Day A7 — Right triangle trigonometric ratios and exact values

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 6: G.SRT.7, G.SRT.8

**Mini-lesson.** Define sine, cosine, and tangent from side ratios in similar right triangles. Memorize and justify the 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 exact values rather than treating them as magic.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Trig ratios depend only on angle measure, not triangle size.
- sin(theta)=cos(90°-theta).
- Special right triangles produce exact radical values.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** In a right triangle, relative to angle θ, define sin θ.
   **Solution.** sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse.
2. **Problem.** Find sin 30° and cos 60°.
   **Solution.** Both equal 1/2 by the 30-60-90 triangle.
3. **Problem.** Find tan 45°.
   **Solution.** tan 45° = 1 because opposite and adjacent legs are equal in a 45-45-90 triangle.
4. **Problem.** If θ and φ are complementary, how are sin θ and cos φ related?
   **Solution.** They are equal: sin θ = cos φ.
5. **Problem.** Find cos 30°.
   **Solution.** cos 30° = √3/2.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. SOH says sine equals opposite over _____.
2. tan 45° = _____.
3. sin θ = cos(_____ - θ).

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. hypotenuse; 2. 1; 3. 90°

## Day A8 — Solve right triangles and applied trig

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 6: G.SRT.8, G.SRT.9

**Mini-lesson.** Move from definitions to problem solving. Choose between Pythagorean Theorem and trig ratios efficiently, and translate word problems into labeled right triangles.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Angles of elevation and depression create equal alternate interior angles.
- Choose SOH-CAH-TOA based on known and unknown quantities.
- Check that answers are reasonable in context.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** A ladder leans so that it makes a 60° angle with the ground and reaches 10 ft up a wall. How long is the ladder?
   **Solution.** Use sin 60° = opposite/hypotenuse = 10/L. Then L = 10/(√3/2)=20/√3≈11.55 ft.
2. **Problem.** In a right triangle, θ has opposite side 7 and adjacent side 24. Find tan θ.
   **Solution.** tan θ = 7/24.
3. **Problem.** A right triangle has legs 8 and 15. Find the acute angle opposite 8 to the nearest degree.
   **Solution.** Hypotenuse is 17, so sin θ = 8/17. θ ≈ 28°.
4. **Problem.** An angle of elevation to the top of a tower is 35° from a point 40 m away. Find the tower height.
   **Solution.** tan 35° = h/40, so h = 40 tan 35° ≈ 28.0 m.
5. **Problem.** When would you use the Pythagorean Theorem instead of a trig ratio?
   **Solution.** Use it when two side lengths are known in a right triangle and the missing quantity is another side.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Angle of elevation is measured from the _____.
2. tan θ = opposite / _____.
3. The Pythagorean Theorem applies to _____ triangles.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. horizontal; 2. adjacent; 3. right

## Day A9 — Area of triangles, quadrilaterals, and regular polygons

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 7: G.GPE.8, G.MG.1

**Mini-lesson.** Review area as decomposition. Finals like giving one complicated figure that must be broken into familiar pieces.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Coordinate area links algebra and geometry.
- Regular polygons can be split into congruent triangles.
- Choosing a smart decomposition is often the whole problem.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Find the area of a triangle with base 10 and height 7.
   **Solution.** Area = (1/2)(10)(7)=35 square units.
2. **Problem.** Find the area of a rectangle with side lengths 8 and 13.
   **Solution.** Area = 8·13 = 104 square units.
3. **Problem.** A regular hexagon is split into 6 congruent triangles each with area 9. Find the hexagon area.
   **Solution.** Total area = 6·9 = 54 square units.
4. **Problem.** Why does decomposition help with irregular polygons?
   **Solution.** It lets you rewrite a hard figure as a sum or difference of familiar figures whose area formulas you know.
5. **Problem.** A trapezoid has bases 6 and 10 and height 4. Find the area.
   **Solution.** Area = (1/2)(6+10)(4)=32 square units.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Triangle area uses one-half times base times _____.
2. Rectangle area is length times _____.
3. A regular polygon can often be split into congruent _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. height; 2. width; 3. triangles

## Day A10 — Circles, arcs, sectors, and modeling with area

**Time budget:** 14 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 7: G.C.6(+), G.GMD.1(+), G.MG.1

**Mini-lesson.** Weekend day: connect radian measure, arc length, and sector area. These are easy points if the formulas are understood conceptually and not memorized blindly.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Arc length is proportional to radius and angle.
- Sector area is the same fraction of total area as the central angle is of 360° or 2π.
- Circle formulas can be justified by similarity and dissection ideas.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Find the arc length of a 90° sector in a circle of radius 8.
   **Solution.** Arc length is (90/360)(2π·8)=4π.
2. **Problem.** Find the sector area of a 60° sector in a circle of radius 6.
   **Solution.** Sector area is (60/360)(π·6^2)=6π.
3. **Problem.** How many radians is 180°?
   **Solution.** π radians.
4. **Problem.** How many radians is 45°?
   **Solution.** π/4 radians.
5. **Problem.** Why is sector area proportional to central angle?
   **Solution.** Because a sector is the same fraction of the whole circle’s area as its central angle is of the full 360° or 2π turn.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. 360° equals _____ radians.
2. Arc length uses radius times angle when angle is in _____.
3. Sector area is a fraction of the circle’s total _____.

## Day A11 — Volume, surface area, density, displacement, and Cavalieri

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 7: G.GMD.1(+), G.GMD.2(+), G.MG.2, G.MG.3

**Mini-lesson.** Treat three-dimensional formulas as modeling tools. Density and displacement problems often mix geometry with units, which is where many exam mistakes happen.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- Volume formulas need units cubed.
- Density is amount per unit area or per unit volume depending on context.
- Cavalieri’s Principle compares solids with equal cross-sectional areas.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 and height 10.
   **Solution.** V=πr^2h=π(9)(10)=90π cubic units.
2. **Problem.** Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 and height 10.
   **Solution.** V=(1/3)πr^2h=(1/3)(90π)=30π cubic units.
3. **Problem.** A box has base area 12 m^2 and height 5 m. Find volume.
   **Solution.** Volume = base area × height = 12·5 = 60 m^3.
4. **Problem.** A city block has 300 people over 0.5 square miles. Find population density.
   **Solution.** Density = 300 / 0.5 = 600 people per square mile.
5. **Problem.** State Cavalieri’s Principle informally.
   **Solution.** If two solids have equal heights and equal cross-sectional areas at every level, then they have equal volumes.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Cylinder volume is πr^2 times _____.
2. Density is amount per unit _____.
3. Volume units are _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. height; 2. area or volume; 3. cubic

## Day A12 — Angles and segments inside and outside circles

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 8: G.C.1, G.C.2, G.C.3

**Mini-lesson.** Now study circle relationships: chords, tangents, secants, inscribed angles, and polygons in circles. This unit is theorem-heavy, so organize it into a clean chart.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- A radius to a tangent is perpendicular at the point of tangency.
- Inscribed angles intercepting the same arc are equal.
- Central angle measure is twice the corresponding inscribed angle measure.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** What is the measure of an inscribed angle intercepting a 100° arc?
   **Solution.** An inscribed angle measures half its intercepted arc, so it is 50°.
2. **Problem.** What is the measure of a central angle intercepting a 100° arc?
   **Solution.** A central angle has the same measure as its intercepted arc, so it is 100°.
3. **Problem.** A tangent touches a circle at P and OP is a radius. What angle is formed between OP and the tangent?
   **Solution.** 90°, because a radius to a point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.
4. **Problem.** Two inscribed angles intercept the same arc. How are they related?
   **Solution.** They are congruent.
5. **Problem.** Why are all circles similar?
   **Solution.** Any circle can be mapped to any other by a dilation and possibly a rigid motion, since circles differ only by center location and radius.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Inscribed angle measure is half the intercepted _____.
2. A radius is perpendicular to a _____ at the point of tangency.
3. Central angle measure equals intercepted _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. arc; 2. tangent; 3. arc

## Day A13 — Equation of a circle and tangent lines

**Time budget:** 6 hours  
**Curriculum coverage:** Honors Geometry Unit 8: G.GPE.1, G.GPE.2(+), G.GPE.6

**Mini-lesson.** Translate between geometry and algebra. You should be able to read a circle from its equation, graph it, and find a tangent line from the radius relationship.

**3 key concepts to master.**
- (x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2 gives center and radius immediately.
- Completing the square converts general form to center-radius form.
- The tangent line is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

**Practice problems with full solutions.**
1. **Problem.** Write the equation of a circle with center (2,-1) and radius 5.
   **Solution.** (x-2)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 25.
2. **Problem.** Give the center and radius of (x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2 = 16.
   **Solution.** Center = (-3,4), radius = 4.
3. **Problem.** Does the point (5,-1) lie on the circle (x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=25?
   **Solution.** Substitute: (5-2)^2+(-1+1)^2 = 3^2+0=9, not 25. So the point is inside the circle, not on it.
4. **Problem.** Find the slope of the radius from center (0,0) to point (3,4). Then find the slope of the tangent there.
   **Solution.** Radius slope = 4/3. Tangent slope = -3/4 because tangent and radius are perpendicular.
5. **Problem.** Why might you complete the square in a circle problem?
   **Solution.** To convert general form into center-radius form so the graph and geometry become easy to read.

**Diagnostic (pass = 2/3).**
1. Center-radius form is (x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=_____.
2. A tangent line is perpendicular to the _____.
3. Completing the square helps reveal the circle’s _____.

**Diagnostic answers:** 1. r^2; 2. radius; 3. center and radius
