# The Altitude Ladder
## Framework 15 — Tracking Your Operating Level
### Author: Mahmoud
### Created: March 20, 2026

---

## Purpose

Track your current operating altitude across two dimensions: content knowledge and research capability. Plan the ascent so that every test, competition, and application deadline falls BELOW your training floor.

The core principle: always train at least one level above where you're being tested. The test becomes the easy part. The training is where you actually learn.

---

## The Levels

### Level 0 — Foundations
**Content:** Basic algebra, counting, elementary number theory, intro geometry.
**Source material:** AoPS Introduction series (Algebra, C&P, Number Theory, Geometry).
**Problems at this level:** AMC 8, AMC 10, Alcumus.
**What it feels like:** "I'm learning the building blocks."
**You're here when:** You're working through AoPS Intro books.

### Level 1 — Intermediate Competition
**Content:** Polynomial manipulation, intermediate counting/probability, modular arithmetic, Euclidean geometry, trigonometry, complex numbers.
**Source material:** AoPS Intermediate series, AMC 12 / AIME problem sets.
**Problems at this level:** AMC 12, AIME 1-10.
**What it feels like:** "I can chain 2-3 ideas together."
**You're here when:** You can solve AMC 12 problems reliably and AIME 1-7 with some effort.

### Level 2 — Olympiad Foundations
**Content:** Inequalities (AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz, Schur), functional equations, graph theory basics, combinatorial arguments, proof writing (induction, contradiction, construction).
**Source material:** WOOT, Engel "Problem-Solving Strategies," past USAJMO/USAMO P1/P4.
**Problems at this level:** AIME 11-15, USAJMO, easy USAMO.
**What it feels like:** "I need to PROVE things, not just compute."
**You're here when:** You can write a clean proof for USAJMO P1 and attempt P2.

### Level 3 — Advanced Olympiad
**Content:** Projective geometry, generating functions, algebraic number theory basics, advanced combinatorics (extremal, probabilistic method), advanced inequalities.
**Source material:** IMO Shortlist, "The Art and Craft of Problem Solving," past USAMO problems.
**Problems at this level:** USAMO, IMO P1/P4, easy P2/P5.
**What it feels like:** "The technique is familiar but the combination is new."
**You're here when:** You can solve IMO P1/P4 within 90 minutes and make real progress on P2/P5.

### Level 4 — Research Foundations
**Content:** Real analysis (Rudin), abstract algebra (Dummit & Foote), point-set topology, linear algebra over arbitrary fields, measure theory basics.
**Source material:** University courses (KU), MIT OCW 18.100/18.701/18.901.
**Problems at this level:** IMO P2/P5, graduate qualifying exam problems, easy research paper exercises.
**What it feels like:** "The language of mathematics is becoming natural."
**You're here when:** You can read a research paper's theorem statements and understand what they claim, even if you can't follow all the proofs.

### Level 5 — Early Graduate
**Content:** Algebraic topology, Riemannian geometry, functional analysis, representation theory, analytic number theory, harmonic analysis.
**Source material:** Graduate textbooks (Hatcher, do Carmo, Stein-Shakarchi), research paper pipeline.
**Problems at this level:** IMO P3/P6, qualifying exam hard problems, paper exercises.
**What it feels like:** "I can read most papers in my area and follow the arguments."
**You're here when:** You can take a research paper at your YELLOW frontier and decompose it fully using the Paper Decomposition Pipeline.

### Level 6 — Research Active
**Content:** Specialized tools in your chosen areas (Boolean function analysis, additive combinatorics, spectral theory, whatever you've focused on).
**Source material:** Frontier papers, open problem lists, arXiv preprints.
**Problems at this level:** Open conjectures, Erdős problems, paper extensions, original questions.
**What it feels like:** "I can contribute to conversations about open problems."
**You're here when:** You can reduce an open problem to a precise conjecture, produce partial results, and write a clean document that researchers take seriously. (You are HERE on P38, with AI assistance.)

### Level 7 — Frontier
**Content:** You are producing new techniques, not just applying existing ones.
**Source material:** Your own work. Problems nobody else has formulated.
**Problems at this level:** Problems that require new ideas, not new combinations of existing ideas.
**What it feels like:** "I see structures nobody has named yet."
**You're here when:** Your work creates tools that other people use. This is Fields Medal territory.

## How to Use This

1. **Before any test/competition:** Check which level it tests. Are you training at least one level above? If not, adjust your training mix.

2. **When choosing what to study:** Always prefer material one level above your current content level. Don't study Level 0 material when you're ready for Level 1.

3. **When feeling overwhelmed:** Check the ladder. If the material feels impossible, you might be trying to jump two levels. Drop to the YELLOW frontier — one level above current.

4. **When feeling bored:** Check the ladder. If the material feels trivial, you're training below your level. Move up.

5. **Monthly review:** Update your current levels. Are you progressing? Is any dimension stalling?

## The Key Insight

The altitude ladder has two dimensions that can be trained independently:

**Content** (what you know) — trained by courses, textbooks, paper decomposition. Increases ~1 level per year with dedicated study.

**Methodology** (how you attack problems) — trained by the Forge, Taxonomy, Perfect Run, multi-model pipeline. You're already at Level 6 here because of the AI augmentation.

Most students climb both dimensions together, slowly. Your strategy: rocket the methodology to Level 6 using AI, then backfill the content. By the time the content catches up (age 16-17), you're operating at a level that would normally require a PhD.

This is not cheating. The methodology is real. The AI assistance is a tool, like a calculator or a library. The content knowledge, once acquired, makes the methodology even more powerful because you can direct it with deeper understanding.
