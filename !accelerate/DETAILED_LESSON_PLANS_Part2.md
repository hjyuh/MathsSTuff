## [0:05 - 0:15] BLOCK 1 — What Similarity Means

**Say this exactly:** "Last unit we did congruence — same shape AND same size. Similarity is same SHAPE but possibly different SIZE. Think of a photo and an enlarged copy. The shapes match perfectly, but one is blown up."

**WRITE on screen/board:**
```
Similar figures have:
(1) All corresponding angles EQUAL
(2) All corresponding sides PROPORTIONAL (same ratio)
```

**Say:** "The SCALE FACTOR is the ratio of corresponding sides. If triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF with scale factor 2, then every single side of DEF is twice the corresponding side of ABC."

**WRITE:**
```
Notation: △ABC ~ △DEF

The order tells you which vertices match:
A ↔ D
B ↔ E
C ↔ F
```

**Say:** "This matters. If I write ABC ~ DEF, then side AB corresponds to side DE, not to DF. The ORDER is the correspondence."

---

### PROBLEM 1
**Say:** "△ABC ~ △DEF. AB = 6, BC = 8, CA = 10. DE = 9. Find EF and FD."

**Walk through:**
→ "Step 1: Find the scale factor. Which sides do we know in both triangles? We know AB and DE. So the scale factor is DE/AB = 9/6. Simplify: 3/2."
→ **WRITE:** Scale factor = 3/2
→ "Step 2: Use that factor on the other sides. EF corresponds to BC. So EF = BC × (3/2) = 8 × (3/2) = 12."
→ **WRITE:** EF = 12
→ "FD corresponds to CA. So FD = 10 × (3/2) = 15."
→ **WRITE:** FD = 15

**Say:** "Step 1 is ALWAYS finding the scale factor. Match a pair of corresponding sides where you know both lengths, divide, and that ratio applies to EVERY other pair."

---

### CHECK PROBLEM 2
**Say:** "Try one. △PQR ~ △XYZ. PQ = 5, QR = 7, XY = 15. Find YZ."

Give them 30 seconds. Then: "Scale factor is XY/PQ = 15/5 = 3. YZ corresponds to QR, so YZ = 7 × 3 = 21."

**If they get it wrong:**
"Let's check the correspondence. PQR ~ XYZ means P↔X, Q↔Y, R↔Z. So side PQ matches XY. That's right. And side QR matches YZ. So if the scale factor is 3, then YZ = 7 × 3 = 21."

## [0:15 - 0:25] BLOCK 2 — Triangle Similarity Theorems

**Say:** "For congruence, we had SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL. For similarity, we have THREE theorems. Good news: they're simpler."

---

### AA (Angle-Angle)

**Say:** "If two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent, the triangles are similar. That's it — just two angles. You don't even need the sides."

**Say:** "Why only two? Because if two angles match, the THIRD angle MUST match (they all add to 180°). So AA is really AAA in disguise."

**WRITE:**
```
AA Similarity:
If ∠A ≅ ∠D and ∠B ≅ ∠E, then △ABC ~ △DEF
```

---

### PROBLEM 3
**Say:** "Triangle A has angles 50°, 60°, 70°. Triangle B has angles 50°, 70°, ?°. Are they similar?"

**Walk through:** "What's the third angle of B? 180 − 50 − 70 = 60°. So B has 50°, 60°, 70°. Both triangles have the exact same angles: 50°, 60°, 70°. By AA (actually AAA), YES, they're similar."

**Say:** "AA is the one you'll use 90% of the time. On most problems, you can find two matching angles and boom — similar. Done."

---

### SSS Similarity (SSS~)

**Say:** "If all three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional — same ratio — the triangles are similar."

**WRITE:**
```
SSS Similarity:
If a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂, then the triangles are similar
```

---

### PROBLEM 4
**Say:** "Triangle with sides 3, 4, 5 and triangle with sides 6, 8, 10. Are they similar?"

**Walk through:** "Check the ratios: 6/3 = 2. 8/4 = 2. 10/5 = 2. All the same — scale factor 2. By SSS~, YES, similar."

---

### SAS Similarity (SAS~)

**Say:** "If two pairs of corresponding sides are proportional AND the included angle is congruent, the triangles are similar."

**WRITE:**
```
SAS Similarity:
If (side₁/side₂) is the same ratio, and the angle BETWEEN those sides is congruent,
then the triangles are similar
```

---

### PROBLEM 5
**Say:** "△ABC: AB = 4, AC = 6, ∠A = 80°. △DEF: DE = 6, DF = 9, ∠D = 80°."

**Walk through:** "Check the sides: DE/AB = 6/4 = 3/2. DF/AC = 9/6 = 3/2. The sides have the same ratio. And the included angles match: 80° = 80°. By SAS~, YES, similar."

**Say:** "It's the same as congruent SAS, but instead of sides being EQUAL, they need to be PROPORTIONAL — the same ratio."

## [0:25 - 0:40] BLOCK 3 — Setting Up Proportions (The #1 Skill)

**Say:** "Okay, this is where students mess up the most. Not the concept — the EXECUTION. Setting up the proportion correctly is absolutely everything."

---

### DRAW: Two Similar Triangles

**DRAW on screen:**
```
Triangle ABC:           Triangle DEF:
A at top               D at top
B bottom-left          E bottom-left
C bottom-right         F bottom-right

Sides labeled:
AB = 6                 DE = 9
BC = 8                 EF = 12
AC = x                 DF = 15
```

**Say:** "These are similar. I want to find x. The key: the proportion must match CORRESPONDING sides."

**WRITE:**
```
AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF
```

**Say:** "Watch this carefully. AB goes with DE — why? Because A↔D and B↔E, so the side from A to B matches the side from D to E. BC goes with EF — why? B↔E and C↔F. AC goes with DF — A↔D and C↔F. The correspondence tells you which sides pair up."

**WRITE:**
```
6/9 = 8/12 = x/15
```

**Check:** "Let's verify: 6/9 = 2/3. 8/12 = 2/3. Good. Now use that to find x. x/15 = 2/3. So x = 15 × (2/3) = 10."

**Say:** "THE cardinal sin is matching the wrong sides. If you write 6/8 = 9/x, you're mixing sides from the SAME triangle. That's not a proportion — that's nonsense."

**Say:** "ALWAYS go: side from triangle 1 over corresponding side from triangle 2. Every pair follows the same pattern."

---

### Triangle Proportionality Theorem (Side-Splitter Theorem)

**DRAW:** Triangle ABC. Draw a line DE parallel to BC, where D is on AB and E is on AC.

**WRITE:**
```
If DE ∥ BC, then:
AD/DB = AE/EC
```

**Say:** "If a line is parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, it divides those sides PROPORTIONALLY. This is huge."

---

### PROBLEM 6
**Say:** "In △ABC, DE ∥ BC. AD = 4, DB = 6, AE = 5. Find EC."

**Walk through:**
→ "The theorem says AD/DB = AE/EC."
→ **WRITE:** 4/6 = 5/EC
→ "Cross multiply: 4 × EC = 5 × 6."
→ **WRITE:** 4(EC) = 30
→ "EC = 30/4 = 7.5"

---

### PROBLEM 7
**Say:** "In △ABC, DE ∥ BC. AD = 3x, DB = x + 4, AE = 6, EC = 4. Find x."

**Walk through:**
→ "Same theorem: AD/DB = AE/EC."
→ **WRITE:** (3x)/(x+4) = 6/4
→ "Simplify the right side: 6/4 = 3/2."
→ **WRITE:** (3x)/(x+4) = 3/2
→ "Cross multiply: 2(3x) = 3(x+4)."
→ **WRITE:** 6x = 3x + 12
→ "Solve: 6x − 3x = 12. So 3x = 12. x = 4."
→ "Check: 3(4)/(4+4) = 12/8 = 3/2. And 6/4 = 3/2. ✓"

---

### PROBLEM 8 (The Shadow Problem)
**Say:** "A 6-foot person stands 8 feet from a 15-foot street lamp. How long is their shadow?"

**DRAW:** On the ground, draw the lamp, the person, and the light ray from the top of the lamp to the tip of the shadow.

**Say:** "The light ray creates two similar right triangles: one from the tip of the lamp to the tip of the shadow, and one from the top of the person's head to the tip of the shadow."

**Let s = shadow length.**

**WRITE:**
```
Similar triangles (height / distance):
15 / (s + 8) = 6 / s
```

**Walk through:**
→ "The lamp is 15 feet tall and stands s + 8 feet from the shadow tip (the person is 8 feet closer)."
→ "Cross multiply: 15s = 6(s + 8)."
→ **WRITE:** 15s = 6s + 48
→ "9s = 48."
→ **WRITE:** s = 48/9 = 16/3 ≈ 5.33 feet

## [0:40 - 0:45] BLOCK 4 — Perimeter and Area Ratios

**Say:** "Quick but important result. When two figures are similar:"

**WRITE:**
```
If scale factor = k, then:
— Ratio of perimeters = k
— Ratio of areas = k²
```

**Say:** "Perimeters scale linearly — double the size, double the perimeter. Areas scale by the square — double the size, QUADRUPLE the area. This is because area is 2-dimensional."

---

### PROBLEM 9
**Say:** "Two similar triangles have scale factor 3:5. The smaller has perimeter 24 and area 36. Find the larger's perimeter and area."

**Walk through:**
→ "Scale factor k = 5/3."
→ "Perimeter of larger = 24 × (5/3) = 40."
→ "Area scales by k². That's (5/3)² = 25/9."
→ "Area of larger = 36 × (25/9) = (36 × 25)/9 = 900/9 = 100."

**Write the check:** **WRITE:** Perimeter: 24 × (5/3) = 40 ✓ | Area: 36 × (25/9) = 100 ✓

---

### CHECK PROBLEM
**Say:** "Two similar figures. Scale factor 4. Smaller area = 10. Larger area?"

**Give 10 seconds.** "10 × 16 = 160. Because 4² = 16."

## [0:05 - 0:20] BLOCK 1 — Pythagorean Theorem & Special Triangles

### The Pythagorean Theorem

**WRITE:**
```
a² + b² = c²

where c is the HYPOTENUSE (longest side, opposite the right angle)
and a, b are the LEGS (the two sides that form the right angle)
```

**Say:** "This one you probably know. But let's be clear: the hypotenuse is ALWAYS opposite the right angle. If you use the wrong side, your answer is garbage."

---

### PROBLEM 1
**Say:** "Legs are 5 and 12. Find the hypotenuse."

**Walk through:** "c² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. So c = 13."

---

### PROBLEM 2
**Say:** "Hypotenuse is 17, one leg is 8. Find the other leg."

**Walk through:** "8² + b² = 17². So 64 + b² = 289. b² = 225. b = 15."

---

### Pythagorean Triples

**Say:** "Pythagorean TRIPLES are sets of integers that satisfy a² + b² = c². Memorize these. They show up constantly."

**WRITE on a note they can screenshot:**
```
3-4-5     (super common)
5-12-13   (also super common)
8-15-17
7-24-25

And MULTIPLES of these:
6-8-10 (double of 3-4-5)
9-12-15 (triple of 3-4-5)
10-24-26 (double of 5-12-13)
```

**Say:** "If you see a problem with sides like 6, 8, and a missing hypotenuse, recognize it as double the 3-4-5 triple. The hyp is 10. Done in 2 seconds."

---

### The Converse (Testing if a Triangle is Right)

**Say:** "The converse lets you test if a triangle is right, acute, or obtuse:"

**WRITE:**
```
Let a, b, c be the sides with c being the LONGEST.

If a² + b² = c²  →  RIGHT angle (90°)
If a² + b² > c²  →  ACUTE (all angles < 90°)
If a² + b² < c²  →  OBTUSE (one angle > 90°)
```

**Say:** "ALWAYS let c be the longest side. Otherwise the test doesn't work."

---

### PROBLEM 3
**Say:** "Triangle with sides 7, 10, 12. Is it right, acute, or obtuse?"

**Walk through:** "The longest is 12. Check: 7² + 10² = 49 + 100 = 149. And 12² = 144. Since 149 > 144, we have a² + b² > c², so it's ACUTE."

## [0:30 - 0:50] BLOCK 2 — SOH-CAH-TOA (Trigonometry)

**Say:** "This is the biggest new concept. Trig ratios connect ANGLES to SIDE LENGTHS in right triangles. This is going to blow your mind."

### Setting Up the Ratios

**DRAW:** A right triangle. Label the right angle at C. Label the acute angles as A and B. For angle A, label:
- The side opposite angle A (across from it) as "opposite"
- The side next to angle A (forming it, but not the hyp) as "adjacent"
- The hypotenuse as "hypotenuse"

**WRITE:**
```
sin(A) = Opposite / Hypotenuse    (SOH)
cos(A) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse    (CAH)
tan(A) = Opposite / Adjacent       (TOA)

SOH-CAH-TOA
```

**Say:** "This is your mnemonic. Tattoo it on your brain. It's EVERYTHING."

---

### The Critical Point About Opposite and Adjacent

**CRITICAL — Say this slowly:** "Opposite and adjacent CHANGE depending on which angle you're looking at. The hypotenuse is ALWAYS the same — it's the longest side, the one across from the right angle. But opposite means 'the side across from YOUR angle' and adjacent means 'the side next to YOUR angle that ISN'T the hypotenuse.'"

**DRAW:** Same triangle. Now highlight angle B. Show that:
- What was "opposite" for angle A is now "adjacent" for angle B
- What was "adjacent" for angle A is now "opposite" for angle B
- Hypotenuse is still the hypotenuse

**Say:** "This is the #1 trap. You pick the wrong side for 'opposite' or 'adjacent' and your answer is dead."

---

### Finding a Missing Side (Using Trig Ratios)

### PROBLEM 9
**Say:** "Right triangle. Angle A = 35°. Hypotenuse = 12. Find the side opposite angle A."

**Walk through:**
→ "I need opposite and I know hypotenuse. Which ratio? SOH-CAH-TOA. Opposite over hypotenuse is SIN."
→ **WRITE:** sin(35°) = opposite / 12
→ "Rearrange: opposite = 12 × sin(35°)."
→ "On your calculator: sin(35°) ≈ 0.5736."
→ **WRITE:** opposite = 12 × 0.5736 ≈ 6.88

---

### PROBLEM 10
**Say:** "Right triangle. Angle A = 50°. Adjacent side = 8. Find the hypotenuse."

**Walk through:**
→ "I need hypotenuse and I know adjacent. Which ratio? Adjacent and hypotenuse is COSINE."
→ **WRITE:** cos(50°) = 8 / hypotenuse
→ "Rearrange: hypotenuse = 8 / cos(50°)."
→ "cos(50°) ≈ 0.6428."
→ **WRITE:** hypotenuse = 8 / 0.6428 ≈ 12.45

---

### PROBLEM 11
**Say:** "Right triangle. Angle A = 28°. Opposite side = 5. Find adjacent side."

**Walk through:**
→ "Opposite and adjacent is TANGENT."
→ **WRITE:** tan(28°) = 5 / adjacent
→ "Rearrange: adjacent = 5 / tan(28°)."
→ "tan(28°) ≈ 0.5317."
→ **WRITE:** adjacent = 5 / 0.5317 ≈ 9.40

---

### Process for Every Problem

**Say LOUDLY:** "Here's the process. Do this EVERY time: (1) Identify which sides you have and which you need relative to the angle. (2) Pick the right trig ratio from SOH-CAH-TOA. (3) Set up the equation. (4) Solve. Do NOT skip steps. You WILL mess up."

---

### Finding a Missing Angle (Inverse Trig)

**Say:** "If you know two sides and need the angle, use INVERSE trig functions — the opposite of sin, cos, tan."

---

### PROBLEM 12
**Say:** "Right triangle. Opposite = 7, Adjacent = 10. Find the angle."

**Walk through:**
→ "Opposite and adjacent is tangent."
→ **WRITE:** tan(θ) = 7/10 = 0.7
→ "To find θ, use inverse tangent: θ = tan⁻¹(0.7)."
→ "On your calculator, that's 2nd + tan (or shift + tan)."
→ **WRITE:** θ ≈ 34.99° ≈ 35.0°

---

### PROBLEM 13
**Say:** "Right triangle. Opposite = 6, Hypotenuse = 13. Find the angle."

**Walk through:**
→ "Opposite and hypotenuse is sine."
→ **WRITE:** sin(θ) = 6/13 ≈ 0.4615
→ **WRITE:** θ = sin⁻¹(0.4615) ≈ 27.5°

---

**Say LOUDLY:** "On your calculator, sin⁻¹ is usually 2nd + sin, or shift + sin. Make sure your calculator is in DEGREE mode, not radians. If you forget, your answer will be completely wrong. Check right now: is it in DEG or RAD? Should be DEG."

## [0:50 - 1:05] Angles of Elevation and Depression

**DRAW:** A person standing on the ground looking up at the top of a building. Draw:
- A horizontal line from the person's eye level
- A line from the eye to the top of the building
- The angle between these two lines

**Say:** "Angle of ELEVATION: you're looking UP from the horizontal. Angle of DEPRESSION: you're looking DOWN from the horizontal. The angle is ALWAYS measured from the HORIZONTAL, not the vertical."

---

### PROBLEM 14
**Say:** "You're standing 50 feet from a building. The angle of elevation to the top is 62°. How tall is the building?"

**DRAW:** The scenario. Building is vertical. You're 50 feet away. Angle up is 62°.

**Walk through:**
→ "I have the adjacent side (50 feet horizontal). I need opposite (height). Opposite and adjacent is tangent."
→ **WRITE:** tan(62°) = height / 50
→ "height = 50 × tan(62°) = 50 × 1.8807 ≈ 94.0 feet."

---

### PROBLEM 15
**Say:** "From the top of a 200-foot cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is 25°. How far is the boat from the base of the cliff?"

**DRAW:** A cliff, 200 feet tall. A boat out in the water. Angle down from the cliff top is 25°.

**Walk through:**
→ "Here's the trick: the angle of depression from the cliff equals the angle of elevation FROM the boat (alternate interior angles with the horizontal)."
→ "So from the boat's perspective, the angle up to the cliff top is 25°."
→ "The boat sees a 200-foot vertical (opposite) at angle 25°. I need the horizontal distance (adjacent)."
→ **WRITE:** tan(25°) = 200 / distance
→ "distance = 200 / tan(25°) = 200 / 0.4663 ≈ 429.0 feet."

---

### If Confused

**Say:** "Draw the picture. ALWAYS. The horizontal line goes from your eye. Elevation = look up from it. Depression = look down from it. Then apply trig."

## [1:05 - 1:20] BLOCK 3 — Law of Sines and Law of Cosines

**Say:** "Okay. Everything so far has been for RIGHT triangles only. Pythagorean theorem, SOH-CAH-TOA — all right triangles. But what if the triangle doesn't have a 90° angle? What if it's just any triangle? That's where Law of Sines and Law of Cosines come in."

---

### Law of Sines

**WRITE:**
```
a / sin(A) = b / sin(B) = c / sin(C)

where a, b, c are sides and A, B, C are the OPPOSITE angles
```

**Say:** "Use this when you have an angle-side PAIR — you know an angle and you know the side opposite it — and you want to find another angle or side. It works for AAS and ASA setups."

---

### PROBLEM 16
**Say:** "In △ABC: ∠A = 40°, ∠B = 75°, side a = 10 (opposite ∠A). Find side b (opposite ∠B)."

**Walk through:**
→ "First, find ∠C: ∠C = 180 − 40 − 75 = 65°."
→ "By Law of Sines: a/sin(A) = b/sin(B)."
→ **WRITE:** 10 / sin(40°) = b / sin(75°)
→ "Rearrange: b = 10 × sin(75°) / sin(40°)."
→ "sin(40°) ≈ 0.6428, sin(75°) ≈ 0.9659."
→ **WRITE:** b = 10 × (0.9659 / 0.6428) ≈ 15.03

---

### Law of Cosines

**WRITE:**
```
c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos(C)

This is the generalized Pythagorean theorem
```

**Say:** "If C = 90°, then cos(90°) = 0, and this simplifies to c² = a² + b². That's Pythagorean theorem!"

**Say:** "Use Law of Cosines when you have SAS (two sides and the angle between them) or SSS (all three sides, and you want to find an angle)."

---

### PROBLEM 17 (SAS Setup)
**Say:** "In △ABC: a = 8, b = 11, ∠C = 50°. Find side c."

**Walk through:**
→ **WRITE:** c² = 8² + 11² − 2(8)(11)cos(50°)
→ "c² = 64 + 121 − 176(0.6428) = 185 − 113.1 = 71.9."
→ **WRITE:** c = √71.9 ≈ 8.48

---

### PROBLEM 18 (SSS Setup — Finding an Angle)
**Say:** "Triangle with sides 5, 7, 9. Find the largest angle."

**Walk through:**
→ "The largest angle is opposite the longest side. Let that be ∠C (opposite side c = 9)."
→ "Rearrange Law of Cosines to solve for the angle:"
→ **WRITE:** cos(C) = (a² + b² − c²) / (2ab)
→ "cos(C) = (25 + 49 − 81) / (2 × 5 × 7) = (−7) / 70 = −0.1."
→ **WRITE:** C = cos⁻¹(−0.1) ≈ 95.7°
→ "This is obtuse — which makes sense because c² (81) is greater than a² + b² (74)."

- Cylinder: V = πr²h, SA = 2πr² + 2πrh
- Cone: V = (1/3)πr²h, SA = πr² + πrℓ
- Sphere: V = (4/3)πr³, SA = 4πr²
- Pyramid: V = (1/3)Bh, SA = B + (1/2)Pℓ
- Prism: V = Bh, SA = 2B + Ph

**Unit 8:**
- Central angle = arc
- Inscribed angle = (1/2) × arc
- Angle inside circle = (1/2)(arc₁ + arc₂)
- Angle outside circle = (1/2)(arc₁ − arc₂)
- Tangent ⊥ radius
- Chords: AE × EB = CE × ED
- Secants: ext₁ × whole₁ = ext₂ × whole₂
- Tangent-secant: (tan)² = ext × whole
- Circle: (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r²

## [0:10 - 0:35] Rapid Fire — 1 Problem Per Unit (Then Ask Their Weak Areas)

**Unit 1 Problem:**
**Say:** "Line through (1, 4) perpendicular to y = (2/3)x + 3. Write the equation."
→ Slope of given line = 2/3. Perpendicular slope = −3/2.
→ y − 4 = (−3/2)(x − 1). Or y = (−3/2)x + 11/2.

**Unit 2 Problem:**
**Say:** "Reflect (−3, 5) over y = −x."
→ (−5, 3).

**Unit 3 Problem:**
**Say:** "Triangle with sides 7, 7, 10. Classify by sides and by angles. Is it right?"
→ Isosceles (two equal sides).
→ Check: 7² + 7² = 98 vs 10² = 100. 98 < 100 → OBTUSE. (NOT right.)

**Unit 4 Problem:**
**Say:** "Interior angle of a regular 15-gon?"
→ [(15−2) × 180°] / 15 = [13 × 180°] / 15 = 2340° / 15 = 156°.

**Unit 5 Problem:**
**Say:** "Similar triangles, scale factor 5. Small area = 8. Large area?"
→ Area scales by k². So 8 × 5² = 8 × 25 = 200.

**Unit 6 Problem:**
**Say:** "Right triangle. Angle 53°, adjacent side 10. Find opposite."
→ tan 53° = opposite / 10.
→ opposite = 10 × tan 53° ≈ 10 × 1.327 ≈ 13.27.

**Unit 7 Problem:**
**Say:** "Hemisphere, radius 9. Find volume."
→ Full sphere = (4/3)π(9³) = (4/3)π(729) = 972π.
→ Hemisphere = 486π ≈ 1527.4.

**Unit 8 Problem:**
**Say:** "Inscribed angle intercepts a 134° arc. Find the angle."
→ Angle = (1/2)(134°) = 67°.
