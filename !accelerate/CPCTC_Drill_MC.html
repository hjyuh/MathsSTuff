CPCTC Drill — Multiple-Choice the Hard Parts

For each proof, the 2 genuinely hard decisions are multiple choice. Pick your answer, then reveal. If you get both MCs right, writing the full proof is almost mechanical — try it, then reveal the solution to check.

Proof 1 — "Bowtie" with a midpoint

Given: Segments AB and CD intersect at point M. M is the midpoint of AB. ∠A ≅ ∠B.
Prove: CM ≅ DM
Red ticks: AM ≅ MB (midpoint). Purple arcs at A and B: ∠A ≅ ∠B (given). The two segments cross at M.
MC-1A. Which two triangles contain the pieces you need to prove CM ≅ DM?
  • a) △ABC and △ABD
  • b) △ACM and △BDM
  • c) △ACD and △BCD
  • d) △AMD and △BMC
Reveal answer

(b) △ACM and △BDM. CM lives in △ACM (vertices A, C, M). DM lives in △BDM (vertices B, D, M). Rule of thumb: the segment you want to prove congruent must be an actual side of the triangle you pick.

Why not (d) △AMD and △BMC? Those triangles don't exist as distinct figures in this bowtie — AMD would require a segment from A to D, which isn't drawn.

MC-1B. You have three pieces: AM ≅ BM, ∠A ≅ ∠B, and one more you need to spot from the figure. Which theorem will prove △ACM ≅ △BDM?
  • a) SSS
  • b) SAS
  • c) ASA
  • d) AAS
  • e) HL
Reveal answer

(c) ASA. Count first: ∠A, ∠B are 1 angle pair. The hidden third piece is ∠AMC ≅ ∠BMD (vertical angles) — that's a second angle pair. Plus AM ≅ BM. That's 2 angles + 1 side.

Is it ASA or AAS? Side AM connects A and M. One angle is at A, the other is at M. The side connects both angle vertices → included → ASA.

Proof 2 — Overlapping triangles with a shared angle

Given: In △ABC, point D is on side AB and point E is on side AC. AB ≅ AC. AD ≅ AE.
Prove: BE ≅ CD
Red single ticks: AB ≅ AC. Blue double-ticks: AD ≅ AE. Purple arc at A highlights ∠A. We want BE ≅ CD (the red crossing segments).
MC-2A. Which two triangles contain BE and CD?
  • a) △ABE and △ACD
  • b) △ABC and △ADE
  • c) △BDE and △CED
  • d) △ABD and △ACE
Reveal answer

(a) △ABE and △ACD.

  • BE is a side of △ABE (vertices A, B, E). ✓
  • CD is a side of △ACD (vertices A, C, D). ✓

Tempting trap: (d) △ABD and △ACE contain AD/AE and AB/AC, but they do not contain BE or CD. CPCTC rule — the triangles must contain the pieces you're asked to prove congruent.

MC-2B. The third piece you need (beyond AB ≅ AC and AD ≅ AE) is hiding in the figure. What is it, and which theorem does it unlock?
  • a) ∠B ≅ ∠C, giving AAS
  • b) BD ≅ CE, giving SSS
  • c) ∠A ≅ ∠A (reflexive / common angle), giving SAS
  • d) DE ∥ BC, giving ASA
Reveal answer

(c) ∠A ≅ ∠A (reflexive / common angle), giving SAS.

Both triangles share the angle at vertex A. This is the reflexive property on an angle — same idea as reflexive on a side, just applied to an angle that both triangles share.

Count: AB ≅ AC (side), AD ≅ AE (side), ∠A ≅ ∠A (angle). 2 sides + 1 angle. The angle's vertex is A, and both sides (AB, AC match with AD, AE) emanate from A → angle is included → SAS.

Key takeaway: reflexive is not just for sides. When two triangles share a vertex and the angle at that vertex is part of both triangles, you can use ∠X ≅ ∠X as the third piece.

Reveal full two-column proof (try writing it first)

Vertex correspondence for △ABE ≅ △ACD: A↔A, B↔C, E↔D. So AB pairs with AC, AE pairs with AD, and BE pairs with CD. ✓

Pattern to internalize

Every CPCTC drill you do is building the same two reflexes:

  1. Triangle-finder reflex: the pair you're asked to prove congruent must be actual sides (or angles) of the two triangles you pick. If the pair isn't in those triangles, you picked wrong triangles.
  2. Hidden-piece reflex: you'll almost always have 2 given pieces, and the 3rd piece hides in the figure. The usual suspects:
    • Vertical angles (two lines crossing → a pair of equal angles)
    • Reflexive side (shared segment = both triangles have it)
    • Reflexive angle (shared vertex-angle = both triangles have it)
    • Alternate interior angles (parallel lines cut by a transversal)
    • Right angles (perpendicular → two 90° angles = congruent)
    • Midpoint → two congruent halves
    • Angle bisector → two congruent sub-angles

When both reflexes fire automatically, CPCTC proofs become 4-line pattern completion. That's the goal.
