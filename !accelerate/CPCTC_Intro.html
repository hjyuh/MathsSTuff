CPCTC — The 3-Step Skeleton

Core idea

CPCTC = Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent.

It is not a congruence theorem (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL are the congruence theorems). CPCTC is what you use after you've already proven two triangles congruent — to conclude that a specific pair of sides or angles (one that wasn't in the givens) is congruent.

Signal that a problem needs CPCTC: the "Prove" statement is about a single pair of segments or a single pair of angles — not about two whole triangles.

  • "Prove AB ≅ CD" → CPCTC job
  • "Prove ∠X ≅ ∠Y" → CPCTC job
  • "Prove △ABC ≅ △DEF" → NOT a CPCTC job (just a regular triangle congruence proof)

The skeleton — every CPCTC proof follows this

  1. Find the two triangles that contain the pair you're asked to prove congruent. (One triangle holds one piece, the other triangle holds the other piece.)
  2. Prove those triangles congruent using SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or HL.
  3. Conclude the pair is congruent by CPCTC.

The entire game is step 1 — spotting which triangles the two pieces live in. Once you've named the triangles, steps 2 and 3 are mechanical.

Worked example

Given: M is the midpoint of AB. M is the midpoint of CD. (So AB and CD cross at M, and M splits each in half.)

Prove: AC ≅ BD

AB and CD cross at M. Red ticks: AM ≅ MB. Blue double-ticks: CM ≅ MD. We want AC ≅ BD (the faint red verticals — they're not literally vertical, just the two pieces we're chasing).

Step 1: Find the triangles

AC lives in △AMC. BD lives in △BMD. Those are our two triangles.

Step 2: Prove △AMC ≅ △BMD

  • AM ≅ MB (M is midpoint of AB, given)
  • CM ≅ MD (M is midpoint of CD, given)
  • ∠AMC ≅ ∠BMD (vertical angles — they're across from each other at M)
  • That's 2 sides + included angle → SAS ✓

Step 3: AC ≅ BD by CPCTC

Practice — try this one tonight

Given: BD bisects ∠ABC. BD bisects ∠ADC.

Prove: AB ≅ CB

BD (green) bisects both ∠ABC and ∠ADC. Prove AB ≅ CB.

Scaffolding questions (answer these before writing the proof):

S1. AB lives in which triangle? CB lives in which triangle? (Those are the two triangles you need.)
S2. What does "BD bisects ∠ABC" give you? What does "BD bisects ∠ADC" give you?
S3. The two triangles share a side. Which side?
S4. Count what you have: how many angles, how many sides? Which congruence theorem fits?

Triangles: △ABD and △CBD.

Notice: 2 angles + the included side BD = ASA. BD is included because it connects vertex B (home of one angle) and vertex D (home of the other angle).

What to remember

  • If the "Prove" targets a single pair of sides or angles → it's a CPCTC problem.
  • Find the two triangles → prove them congruent → invoke CPCTC on the last line. Always.
  • CPCTC is never the reason you use to prove the triangles themselves congruent. It's only for parts, after the triangle-congruence work is already done.
