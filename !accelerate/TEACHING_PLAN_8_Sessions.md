# HONORS GEOMETRY TEACHING PLAN — 8 Sessions
## One Unit Per Call. Teach It, Drill It, Send the Worksheet.

---

## HOW EACH SESSION WORKS (Template)

Every call follows the same rhythm:

```
[0:00 - 0:05]  Vibe check — what do they already know?
[0:05 - 0:30]  TEACH — core concepts, build from zero
[0:30 - 0:45]  LIVE PROBLEMS — do 3-5 together on call
[0:45 - 0:55]  COMMON TRAPS — show them what goes wrong
[0:55 - 1:00]  Wrap up — assign the worksheet, set expectations
```

Adjust timing based on how fast they pick things up. Some units are heavier than others — I've marked estimated difficulty and time below.

## SESSION 1: Foundations of Geometry
**Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆ | Estimated time: 45-60 min**

### Before the Call
- Have open: `Unit1_Foundations_of_Geometry.md`
- Skim the Module 1 problems from `Worksheet_Unit1_Foundations.md` so you have examples ready

### Teaching Goals (what they MUST leave knowing)

**Block 1 — The Language (10 min)**
- [ ] Points, lines, segments, rays — notation (AB̅ vs AB⃗ vs AB↔)
- [ ] Angle types: acute (<90°), right (=90°), obtuse (>90°), straight (=180°)
- [ ] Angle pairs: complementary (sum=90°), supplementary (sum=180°), vertical (equal), linear pair (supplementary + adjacent)
- **Check:** "If two angles are vertical and one is 73°, what's the other?" → 73°. If they get this, move on.

**Block 2 — Parallel Lines + Transversal (10 min)**
- [ ] Draw two parallel lines cut by a transversal. Label ALL 8 angles.
- [ ] Teach the 4 relationships: corresponding (=), alternate interior (=), alternate exterior (=), co-interior/same-side interior (sum=180°)
- [ ] The pattern: angles that look like they match ARE equal; angles on the same side between the lines ADD to 180°
- **Check:** Point to any two angles → they should instantly say "equal" or "supplementary" and name the relationship

**Block 3 — Coordinate Geometry (15 min)**
- [ ] Distance formula: d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²) — "it's just Pythagorean theorem on a coordinate plane"
- [ ] Midpoint formula: M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2) — "just average the x's and average the y's"
- [ ] Slope: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) — "rise over run"
- [ ] Parallel lines → same slope. Perpendicular lines → negative reciprocal slopes (flip and negate)
- [ ] Writing equations: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) — point-slope form is king
- **Check:** "Write the equation of a line through (2, 5) perpendicular to y = 3x + 1" → slope = −⅓, equation: y − 5 = −⅓(x − 2)

**Live Problems Together (10 min)**
Do 3 problems together from the worksheet Module 1/2. Screen share or whiteboard. Make THEM do the work while you guide.

**Traps to Show Them (5 min)**
- Perpendicular slope: it's NEGATIVE reciprocal, not just reciprocal. Slope of 2 → perp is −½, NOT ½
- Distance formula: the negatives get squared anyway, so don't panic about signs
- Co-interior angles SUM to 180° — they don't equal each other

### End of Call
- [ ] Send them `Worksheet_Unit1_Foundations.md`
- [ ] Tell them: "Do Modules 1 and 2. If you get stuck, read the explanations at the bottom. Bring your questions to next call."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can find distance, midpoint, slope, write line equations, and name angle relationships without notes.

## SESSION 2: Transformations
**Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆ | Estimated time: 45-60 min**

### Quick Review (5 min)
Ask 2-3 rapid-fire questions from Unit 1 to check retention. Fix any gaps.

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — What Transformations Are (5 min)**
- [ ] Pre-image → image. Input point → output point. That's it.
- [ ] Rigid motions (translations, reflections, rotations) preserve EVERYTHING — size, shape, angle, distance
- [ ] Dilations change SIZE but keep shape. NOT rigid.

**Block 2 — The Coordinate Rules (15 min)**
This is the meat. Drill these:
- [ ] **Translation:** (x,y) → (x+a, y+b) — just slide it
- [ ] **Reflection over x-axis:** (x,y) → (x, −y) — flip the y
- [ ] **Reflection over y-axis:** (x,y) → (−x, y) — flip the x
- [ ] **Reflection over y=x:** (x,y) → (y, x) — swap them
- [ ] **Reflection over y=−x:** (x,y) → (−y, −x) — swap and negate both
- [ ] **Rotation 90° CCW:** (x,y) → (−y, x)
- [ ] **Rotation 180°:** (x,y) → (−x, −y)
- [ ] **Rotation 270° CCW (= 90° CW):** (x,y) → (y, −x)
- [ ] **Dilation (center origin):** (x,y) → (kx, ky)
- **Check:** "Rotate (3, −2) 90° counterclockwise" → (2, 3). If they can do this cold, they're good.

**Block 3 — Compositions & Congruence (10 min)**
- [ ] Composition = do multiple transformations in order (ORDER MATTERS)
- [ ] If you can map Figure A onto Figure B using ONLY rigid motions → they're congruent
- [ ] Symmetry: line symmetry (fold it), rotational symmetry (spin it and it looks the same)
- **Check:** "Reflect (1,4) over y-axis, then rotate 90° CCW. What do you get?" → (−1,4) → (−4,−1)

**Live Problems (10 min)**
Do 3-4 together from worksheet Module 1/2.

**Traps (5 min)**
- 90° CW ≠ 90° CCW! (CW = 270° CCW)
- Composition order: "reflect then translate" ≠ "translate then reflect"
- Dilation is NOT a rigid motion — if a problem asks about congruence, dilations don't count

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit2_Transformations.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "Do Modules 1-3. Module 3 has traps — see if you can spot them before reading the answers."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can apply any single transformation, do a 2-step composition, and identify symmetry.

## SESSION 3: Triangles — Properties & Congruence
**Difficulty: ★★★★☆ | Estimated time: 60-75 min**

This is the first BIG unit. Budget extra time. This session might run long.

### Quick Review (5 min)
2-3 transformation problems from last session.

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — Triangle Basics (10 min)**
- [ ] Angle sum = 180°. Always. No exceptions. Practice with algebra (2x + 3x + 40 = 180)
- [ ] Exterior Angle Theorem: exterior angle = sum of the two remote interior angles
- [ ] Classification: scalene/isosceles/equilateral (sides) and acute/right/obtuse (angles)
- [ ] Isosceles Triangle Theorem: base angles are congruent (and converse)

**Block 2 — The 5 Congruence Theorems (20 min) ← MOST IMPORTANT**
Teach each one with a clear visual description:
- [ ] **SSS:** all three sides match → congruent
- [ ] **SAS:** two sides and the INCLUDED angle match → congruent
- [ ] **ASA:** two angles and the INCLUDED side match → congruent
- [ ] **AAS:** two angles and a NON-included side match → congruent
- [ ] **HL:** right triangles only — hypotenuse + one leg match → congruent
- [ ] **SSA does NOT work** — show them why (two possible triangles)
- **Check:** Show them a pair of triangles with markings → "Which theorem?" They should answer in under 10 seconds.

**Block 3 — Proofs & CPCTC (15 min)**
- [ ] Two-column proof format: LEFT = statements, RIGHT = reasons
- [ ] The flow: Given info → find congruent parts → name the theorem → conclude congruence → use CPCTC for specific parts
- [ ] CPCTC = "Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent" — it's the LAST step, after you've proven the triangles congruent
- [ ] Walk through ONE complete proof together, line by line
- **Check:** Give them a proof setup → they write at least the congruence statement and name the theorem

**Block 4 — Special Segments (5 min, brief overview)**
- [ ] Midsegment: connects midpoints of two sides → parallel to third side, half its length
- [ ] Know the four centers exist: centroid (medians), incenter (angle bisectors), circumcenter (perp bisectors), orthocenter (altitudes)

**Live Problems (10 min)**
Do 2 proofs together. Make them lead.

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit3_Triangles.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "Do Modules 1-2 AND attempt at least 2 proofs from Module 5. Proofs are the hardest part — the only way to get good is to do them."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can identify the correct congruence theorem, understand CPCTC, and write a basic 5-step proof.

## SESSION 4: Polygons & Quadrilaterals
**Difficulty: ★★★☆☆ | Estimated time: 50-65 min**

### Quick Review (5 min)
Show two triangles with markings → "Which congruence theorem?" Do 3 of these rapid-fire.

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — Polygon Angle Formulas (10 min)**
- [ ] Interior angle sum = (n−2) × 180°. "A triangle has 180°, each additional side adds another 180°"
- [ ] Each angle of a regular polygon = (n−2) × 180° / n
- [ ] Exterior angle sum = 360° always (for convex polygons)
- [ ] Each exterior angle of regular polygon = 360°/n
- **Check:** "Interior angle sum of an octagon?" → (8−2)×180 = 1080°. "Each angle if regular?" → 1080/8 = 135°

**Block 2 — The Quadrilateral Hierarchy (20 min) ← THE CORE**

Draw this family tree together:
```
         Quadrilateral
              |
          Trapezoid
              |
        Parallelogram
         /         \
    Rectangle    Rhombus
         \         /
           Square
```
(Kite is separate, branching from Quadrilateral)

For each shape, teach the properties:
- [ ] **Parallelogram:** opp sides ∥ & ≅, opp angles ≅, consecutive angles supp, diagonals bisect each other
- [ ] **Rectangle:** parallelogram + all 90° angles + congruent diagonals
- [ ] **Rhombus:** parallelogram + all sides ≅ + diagonals ⊥ + diagonals bisect angles
- [ ] **Square:** BOTH rectangle AND rhombus (every property of both)
- [ ] **Trapezoid:** exactly one pair of parallel sides, midsegment = (b₁+b₂)/2
- [ ] **Isosceles Trap:** base angles ≅, diagonals ≅
- [ ] **Kite:** 2 pairs consecutive ≅ sides, diagonals ⊥, one diagonal bisected, one pair opp angles ≅

The KEY insight to hammer home: "A square IS a rectangle IS a parallelogram. Properties flow DOWN the tree."

**Block 3 — Coordinate Proofs (10 min)**
- [ ] Proving a shape using coordinates: slope (∥), distance (≅), midpoint (bisection)
- [ ] Walk through one example: "Given 4 vertices, prove it's a rectangle"
- **Check:** "What do you calculate to prove sides are parallel?" → slopes. "To prove diagonals bisect each other?" → midpoints of both diagonals are the same point.

**Live Problems (10 min)**
Do 2-3 problems mixing angle formulas + quadrilateral properties.

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit4_Polygons_Quads.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "Draw the hierarchy from memory. Then do Modules 1-2. If you can recreate that tree and list 3 properties of each shape without looking, you own this unit."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can draw the hierarchy, list properties of each quadrilateral, and use angle sum formulas.

## SESSION 5: Similarity
**Difficulty: ★★★☆☆ | Estimated time: 50-60 min**

### Quick Review (5 min)
"Name 3 properties of a rhombus." "Interior angle sum of a decagon?"

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — What Similarity Means (10 min)**
- [ ] Similar = same SHAPE, different SIZE. Angles equal, sides proportional.
- [ ] Scale factor k: the ratio of corresponding sides. If k=2, the similar figure is twice as big.
- [ ] Notation matters: △ABC ~ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Correspondence determines which sides match.
- **Check:** "If △ABC ~ △DEF with scale factor 3, and AB = 4, what's DE?" → 12

**Block 2 — Triangle Similarity Theorems (10 min)**
- [ ] **AA:** Two angles equal → similar. (Most common — and you only need 2 because the third is forced by 180°)
- [ ] **SSS~:** All three pairs of sides proportional → similar
- [ ] **SAS~:** Two pairs of sides proportional AND included angle equal → similar
- **Check:** "Two triangles have angles 40°, 60°, 80° and 40°, 80°, 60°. Similar?" → Yes, AA.

**Block 3 — Setting Up Proportions (15 min) ← WHERE STUDENTS STRUGGLE**
- [ ] ALWAYS write the proportion with triangles labeled: side from △1 / corresponding side from △2
- [ ] Cross multiply and solve
- [ ] Triangle Proportionality Theorem (Side-Splitter): line ∥ to one side cuts the other two proportionally
- [ ] Walk through 3 examples with increasing complexity
- **Check:** Give a diagram with parallel lines creating similar triangles → they set up and solve the proportion

**Block 4 — Ratios (5 min)**
- [ ] If scale factor = k → ratio of perimeters = k, ratio of areas = k²
- [ ] "Scale factor 3 means perimeter is 3× bigger, area is 9× bigger"
- **Check:** "Scale factor is 2, small triangle has area 5. Large triangle area?" → 20

**Live Problems (10 min)**

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit5_Similarity.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "Modules 1-2, and do at least 5 proportion problems. The #1 mistake is matching the wrong sides — be careful with correspondence."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can identify similar triangles, set up proportions correctly, and use the area ratio.

## SESSION 6: Right Triangles & Trigonometry
**Difficulty: ★★★★★ | Estimated time: 70-80 min**

This is the HARDEST session. It's dense. You might need to split this across two calls if they're struggling.

### Quick Review (5 min)
One similarity proportion problem.

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — Pythagorean Theorem & Special Triangles (15 min)**
- [ ] a² + b² = c² — c is ALWAYS the hypotenuse (longest side, across from 90°)
- [ ] Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17 (and multiples like 6-8-10)
- [ ] **45-45-90:** legs equal, hypotenuse = leg × √2. Sides: x, x, x√2
- [ ] **30-60-90:** short leg opposite 30°, long leg = short × √3, hypotenuse = short × 2. Sides: x, x√3, 2x
- [ ] Walk through 2 examples of each special triangle
- **Check:** "A 45-45-90 triangle has hypotenuse 10. Find the legs." → 10/√2 = 5√2

**Block 2 — SOH-CAH-TOA (20 min) ← THE BIG ONE**
- [ ] sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse
- [ ] cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse
- [ ] tan θ = opposite/adjacent
- [ ] CRITICAL: opposite and adjacent DEPEND ON WHICH ANGLE you're looking at. The hypotenuse never changes.
- [ ] Finding a side: set up the ratio, solve. Example: "Given angle 35° and hypotenuse 12, find the opposite side" → sin 35° = x/12 → x = 12 sin 35°
- [ ] Finding an angle: use inverse trig. "tan θ = 7/4 → θ = tan⁻¹(7/4) ≈ 60.3°"
- [ ] Angles of elevation (looking UP) and depression (looking DOWN) — draw the picture every time
- **Check:** Draw a right triangle with a 40° angle and one side labeled → they set up the right trig ratio

**Block 3 — Law of Sines & Cosines (15 min)**
- [ ] These are for NON-right triangles. SOH-CAH-TOA is ONLY for right triangles.
- [ ] **Law of Sines:** a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C → use when you have AAS or ASA
- [ ] **Law of Cosines:** c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C → use when you have SAS or SSS
- [ ] Decision tree: Right triangle? → Pythagorean/SOH-CAH-TOA. Not right + have angle-side pair? → Law of Sines. Not right + have SAS or SSS? → Law of Cosines.
- [ ] Walk through one of each
- **Check:** "You know two sides and the included angle of a non-right triangle. Which law?" → Law of Cosines

**Block 4 — Area = ½ab sin C (5 min)**
- [ ] When you know two sides and the included angle, use A = ½ab sin C
- [ ] Quick example

**Live Problems (10 min)**
Mix: one special right triangle, one SOH-CAH-TOA, one Law of Sines/Cosines.

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit6_Right_Triangles_Trig.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "This is the hardest unit. Do ALL of Modules 1-3. If trig feels shaky, redo Module 1 problems until you can do them without thinking. The decision tree is your best friend."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can solve any right triangle with trig, use special right triangles, and choose the correct law for non-right triangles.

## SESSION 7: Area, Surface Area, Volume
**Difficulty: ★★★☆☆ | Estimated time: 50-60 min**

This unit is formula-heavy but conceptually lighter. It's mostly "plug and chug" — but there are a LOT of formulas.

### Quick Review (5 min)
One trig problem from last session. Make sure it stuck.

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — 2D Area Formulas (10 min)**
Rapid-fire through these. Most they've seen before:
- [ ] Rectangle: A = lw
- [ ] Triangle: A = ½bh
- [ ] Parallelogram: A = bh (NOT side × side — h is the HEIGHT, perpendicular to base)
- [ ] Trapezoid: A = ½(b₁ + b₂)h — "average the bases, multiply by height"
- [ ] Rhombus/Kite: A = ½d₁d₂
- [ ] Regular polygon: A = ½ × apothem × perimeter — "apothem = distance from center to middle of a side"
- [ ] Circle: A = πr² and C = 2πr
- **Check:** "Area of a regular hexagon with side 6?" → They need to find apothem (3√3), perimeter (36), then A = ½(3√3)(36) = 54√3

**Block 2 — Arcs & Sectors (10 min)**
- [ ] Arc length = (θ/360°) × 2πr — "it's a fraction of the circumference"
- [ ] Sector area = (θ/360°) × πr² — "it's a fraction of the full area"
- [ ] The fraction is always θ/360° — "what fraction of the full circle is this slice?"
- **Check:** "Arc length for 120° in a circle with radius 9?" → (120/360)(2π·9) = 6π

**Block 3 — 3D Formulas (15 min)**
Group them logically:
- [ ] PRISMS/CYLINDERS (flat top): V = Bh, SA = 2B + lateral
- [ ] PYRAMIDS/CONES (pointy top): V = ⅓Bh — "a third of the prism/cylinder"
- [ ] SPHERE: V = (4/3)πr³, SA = 4πr²
- [ ] Composite solids: break into pieces, add/subtract volumes
- [ ] Cavalieri's Principle: same cross-section at every height → same volume (even if tilted)
- **Check:** "Volume of a cone with radius 5, height 12?" → ⅓π(25)(12) = 100π

**Block 4 — Density & Displacement (5 min)**
- [ ] Density = mass/volume
- [ ] Displacement: drop object in water → water level rise = object's volume
- Quick example of each

**Live Problems (10 min)**

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit7_Area_SA_Volume.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "Write every formula from memory on a blank page. Check yourself. Redo until you can write them all. Then do Modules 1-2 from the worksheet."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can recall all formulas and handle composite figures (cylinder + hemisphere, etc.)

## SESSION 8: Circles
**Difficulty: ★★★★★ | Estimated time: 65-75 min**

Last unit! This one has a lot of theorems but they follow patterns.

### Quick Review (5 min)
"Volume of a sphere with radius 6?" → (4/3)π(216) = 288π

### Teaching Goals

**Block 1 — Angle Relationships in Circles (20 min) ← THE CORE**

Teach the three cases as ONE pattern:
- [ ] **Central angle** (vertex AT center): angle = arc
- [ ] **Inscribed angle** (vertex ON circle): angle = ½ arc
- [ ] **Angle INSIDE circle** (two chords cross): angle = ½(arc₁ + arc₂)
- [ ] **Angle OUTSIDE circle** (two secants/tangents from external point): angle = ½(far arc − near arc)

The pattern: as the vertex moves from center → circle → outside, the formula goes from full → half → half the difference.

- [ ] Special case: inscribed angle on a diameter = 90° (semicircle = 180° arc, half = 90°)
- **Check:** "An inscribed angle intercepts a 130° arc. What's the angle?" → 65°

**Block 2 — Tangent Lines (10 min)**
- [ ] Tangent is PERPENDICULAR to the radius at the point of tangency → creates a right angle → Pythagorean theorem applies
- [ ] Two tangent segments from the same external point are CONGRUENT
- **Check:** Tangent segment from external point to circle, radius = 5, distance from point to center = 13. Find tangent length. → √(169−25) = 12

**Block 3 — Segment Products (10 min)**
- [ ] **Chords:** (piece₁)(piece₂) = (piece₃)(piece₄)
- [ ] **Secant-Secant:** (external)(whole) = (external)(whole)
- [ ] **Tangent-Secant:** (tangent)² = (external)(whole secant)
- The mnemonic: "NEAR × FAR = NEAR × FAR" from the external point
- **Check:** Two chords intersect. Segments are 3, 8, 4, x. Find x. → 3(8) = 4(x) → x = 6

**Block 4 — Equation of a Circle (10 min)**
- [ ] Standard form: (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r². Center = (h,k), radius = r.
- [ ] Watch the signs: (x−3)² means h = 3 (positive), (x+2)² means h = −2 (negative)
- [ ] Completing the square: group x terms, group y terms, complete square for each, add same value to right side
- [ ] Walk through one completing-the-square example together
- **Check:** "x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0. Find center and radius." → (x−3)² + (y+2)² = 25, center (3,−2), radius 5

**Live Problems (10 min)**
Mix: one inscribed angle, one segment product, one equation of a circle.

### End of Call
- [ ] Send `Worksheet_Unit8_Circles.md`
- [ ] Assignment: "This is the last unit. Do the full worksheet — all modules. Pay extra attention to Module 3 traps. The inscribed angle = ½ arc mistake is the #1 thing people get wrong on finals."
- [ ] **Success metric:** They can handle any angle-in-a-circle problem, set up segment products, and convert circle equations.

## TIPS FOR YOU AS THE TEACHER

**Pacing:** If someone's struggling, slow down. It's better to really nail 70% of a unit than to rush through 100% and retain nothing. The worksheet covers the rest.

**The "do you get it?" trap:** Don't ask "do you understand?" — they'll always say yes. Instead, give them a problem and watch them solve it. Their hands tell you more than their words.

**When they're stuck:** Don't give the answer. Ask leading questions: "What do you know?" → "What are you trying to find?" → "What formula connects those?" This is how it sticks.

**The worksheets are the real teaching tool.** Your call gives them the framework and confidence. The worksheet gives them the reps. The intuitive explanations at the bottom mean they can self-correct when you're not there.

**Energy management:** Sessions 3, 6, and 8 are the hardest. If your friends are fading, it's okay to split any of these into two shorter calls.
