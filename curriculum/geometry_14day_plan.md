## PHILOSOPHY

**This is not a grind plan. It's a deficit-targeted plan.**

1. **Day 1 is a diagnostic.** We don't "study Unit 1" — we test all 8 units with 3-4 problems each to find the real weak spots. The self-assessment above is a starting point, not the final word.

2. **Time is reallocated based on the diagnostic, not distributed evenly.** If Unit 1 is truly solid, it gets 20 min of review. If Unit 5 is a wreck, it gets 6+ hours.

3. **Practice problems, not textbook reading.** You learn geometry by doing geometry, especially proofs. The goal on each unit day: 20+ actual problems, with explanations for the ones you miss.

4. **Proofs are the hardest transferable skill.** Unit 3 sets up Units 4, 5, and 8 proof-wise. Extra time on proofs early pays compound interest.

5. **Two-pass structure.** Days 1-9 build knowledge; Days 10-14 are practice exams + targeted review. Don't skip the second pass; that's where accuracy comes from.

## FORMULAS TO MEMORIZE (print this, tape to wall)

### Coordinate Geometry (Unit 1)
- Distance: d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²)
- Midpoint: ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
- Slope: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
- Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: slopes multiply to −1.

### Transformations (Unit 2)
- Translation: (x,y) → (x+a, y+b)
- Reflection over x-axis: (x,y) → (x, −y)
- Reflection over y-axis: (x,y) → (−x, y)
- Reflection over y=x: (x,y) → (y, x)
- Rotation 90° CCW about origin: (x,y) → (−y, x)
- Rotation 180° about origin: (x,y) → (−x, −y)
- Rotation 270° CCW (= 90° CW) about origin: (x,y) → (y, −x)

### Triangle Congruence (Unit 3)
- **SSS**: three sides equal
- **SAS**: two sides and included angle equal
- **ASA**: two angles and included side equal
- **AAS**: two angles and non-included side equal
- **HL**: right triangles, hypotenuse and one leg equal
- Triangle angle sum: 180°
- Exterior angle = sum of two remote interior angles
- Isosceles: base angles equal

### Quadrilaterals (Unit 4) — hierarchy
- Parallelogram: opposite sides ∥ and ≅, opposite angles ≅, consecutive angles supplementary, diagonals bisect each other
- Rectangle: parallelogram + 4 right angles + diagonals ≅
- Rhombus: parallelogram + 4 sides ≅ + diagonals ⊥ + diagonals bisect angles
- Square: rectangle + rhombus (all of the above)
- Trapezoid: exactly one pair of parallel sides (isosceles: legs ≅, base angles ≅)
- Kite: two pairs of consecutive sides ≅; diagonals ⊥

### Similarity (Unit 5)
- **AA**: two angles equal → triangles similar
- **SSS**: three sides proportional
- **SAS**: two sides proportional + included angle equal
- Triangle proportionality: line ∥ to side cuts other two sides proportionally
- Altitude to hypotenuse of right triangle: creates 3 similar triangles
- Geometric mean: h² = xy, a² = x·c, b² = y·c (where c=hypotenuse, x,y are segments)

### Right Triangle Trig (Unit 6)
- sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj
- 30-60-90: sides in ratio 1 : √3 : 2
- 45-45-90: sides in ratio 1 : 1 : √2
- Law of Sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
- Law of Cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C
- Area of any triangle: ½ab sin C

### Area & Volume (Unit 7)
- Triangle: A = ½bh
- Parallelogram: A = bh
- Trapezoid: A = ½(b₁+b₂)h
- Regular polygon: A = ½ap (apothem × perimeter)
- Circle: A = πr², C = 2πr
- Sector: A = (θ/360)πr², arc length = (θ/360)2πr
- Prism/cylinder volume: V = Bh (base area × height)
- Pyramid/cone volume: V = ⅓Bh
- Sphere volume: V = ⁴⁄₃πr³
- Sphere surface area: SA = 4πr²

### Circles (Unit 8)
- Inscribed angle = ½ intercepted arc
- Central angle = intercepted arc
- Tangent ⊥ radius at point of tangency
- Two tangents from external point are ≅
- Chord-chord angle = ½(sum of arcs)
- Two secants from external point: angle = ½(far arc − near arc)
- Chord-chord power: AE · EB = CE · ED
- Secant-secant from external: whole · outside = whole · outside
- Tangent-secant from external: tangent² = whole · outside
- Equation of circle: (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r²

## HOW TO PRACTICE EFFECTIVELY

**For each problem you miss:**
1. DON'T look at the solution yet. Try it again.
2. If still stuck, read the solution ONCE.
3. Close the solution. Redo the problem from scratch.
4. If you get it right this time, it counts as learned. If not, mark it for review tomorrow.

**Active recall > re-reading.** Never just re-read a textbook. Always test yourself.

**For proofs specifically:**
- Write the "Given" and "Prove" first.
- Draw the figure if not given.
- Work BACKWARDS from "Prove" — what would I need to show just before the final statement?
- Look for the congruence criterion hidden in the given info.
