## 2. First Repair: Right-Core Already Means Semiprime

Fix

```text
H(X) = floor(X^theta),    1/3 < theta < 1/2.
```

Let `n in [X,2X]` satisfy

```text
P^-(n) > H(X).
```

Then for all sufficiently large `X`, `n` is a semiprime.

### Lemma 2.1

If `n in [X,2X]` is composite and `P^-(n) > H(X)`, then `n = pq` with primes
`p,q > H(X)` (possibly `p=q`).

### Proof

If `n` had at least three prime factors counting multiplicity, then

```text
n >= H(X)^3.
```

Since `3 theta > 1`, we have `H(X)^3 > 2X` for all sufficiently large `X`,
contradicting `n <= 2X`. Since `n` is composite, it must therefore have exactly
two prime factors. `QED`

This already applies to the right-core condition used in
`buffered-live-pair-bridge.md`. The extra "deep-core" threshold `Y = A H`
from 5.2 is not doing essential structural work.

In particular, for the `theta = 0.36` longest-core certificate in
`results/right-core-theta036-N1000000.json`, the sampled values are exactly of
this form; for example

```text
934091 = 229 * 4079,
934163 = 577 * 1619,
934169 = 787 * 1187.
```

That is not an accident; it is the asymptotic regime.

## 3. Second Repair: Local Rigidity in an `H`-Window

Once every right-core vertex is a semiprime with both prime factors `> H`, the
local geometry becomes rigid.

### Lemma 3.1

Let `m,n` be distinct integers in an interval of length `< H`, and suppose

```text
P^-(m) > H,    P^-(n) > H.
```

Then `gcd(m,n)=1`.

### Proof

Any common prime divisor `p` would satisfy `p > H` and `p | (m-n)`, but

```text
0 < |m-n| < H < p,
```

impossible. `QED`

### Consequences

1. In one successor window of length `H`, no large prime factor can be reused.
2. A rough-semiprime cluster in a short interval is pairwise coprime.
3. The finite survivor chains seen in the data are not generated by repeatedly
   recycling a small library of prime factors.

This already pushes against a naive CRT construction.

## 4. Third Repair: Why CRT/Block Constructions Do Not Bypass the Gap

The key obstruction is stronger than 5.2 states.

### Lemma 4.1 (Exact-value obstruction)

Let `n in [X,2X]` be right-core for `H(X)=floor(X^theta)`, `1/3<theta<1/2`.
If primes `p,q > H(X)` satisfy `pq | n`, then for all sufficiently large `X`,

```text
n = pq.
```

### Proof

Write `n = p q r`. If `r > 1`, then every prime factor of `r` is also `> H(X)`
because `n` is right-core. Hence `r > H(X)`, so

```text
n >= H(X)^3 > 2X
```

for large `X`, contradiction. Thus `r=1`. `QED`

### Interpretation

In the right-core power-law regime, "force divisibility by `pq`" is not a soft
compositeness condition. It forces the *entire value*.

That kills the usual CRT/block strategy:

- In standard CRT constructions one forces many nearby integers to be divisible
  by prescribed moduli.
- Here, if the prescribed modulus is a product of two `>H` primes, any valid
  right-core hit in `[X,2X]` must equal that exact semiprime.
- So CRT does not manufacture a flexible family of candidate composites; it
  reduces immediately to the short-interval location problem for exact semiprime
  values.

There is a second obstruction. If `p > H`, then an interval of length `H`
contains at most one multiple of `p`. Hence even before using Lemma 4.1, a
fixed large prime can support at most one right-core candidate in a successor
window.

Therefore I do **not** see a genuine deterministic bypass of the analytic gap
inside the present semiprime/right-core framework.

## 5. What the Analytic Problem Really Is

For a buffered right-core source `(u,v)` at scale `X`, write

```text
u = p1 p2,    v = r1 r2,
```

with all four primes `> H(X)`.

Because the candidate window has length `H(X) < p` for every relevant prime
factor `p`, a right-core successor `w` must be of the form

```text
w = a b
```

with primes `a,b > H(X)`, and it must satisfy both:

1. `a` and `b` divide none of the integers in `[u,v]`;
2. the interval `[w, w + H(w)]` contains no multiple of `r1` or `r2`.

So the real successor count is a rough-semiprime count with a moving forbidden
factor set coming from the previous interval. The future-clearance condition is
another moving two-residue exclusion coming from the two prime factors of `v`.

This is more specific than "rough semiprimes in short intervals" and more
honest than the 5.2 wording.

## 6. Why Known Unconditional Tools Do Not Close This

There are unconditional results showing that bare `E_2` numbers are common in
very short intervals, even polylogarithmic ones, for almost all starting points.
Examples:

- Teravaeinen, *Almost Primes in Almost All Short Intervals* (2016):
  [arXiv:1510.06005](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.06005)
- Matomaeki and Teravaeinen, *Almost primes in almost all short intervals II*
  (2023): [arXiv:2207.05038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05038)
- Matomaeki, *Almost primes in almost all very short intervals* (2022):
  [arXiv:2012.11565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11565)

These do **not** close EP1212 here, for three separate reasons.

1. They are almost-all statements, not slab-survival statements.
2. They count unrestricted `E_2` numbers, not right-core semiprimes with
   `P^-(n) > H(X)`.
3. They do not impose the moving two-window avoidance condition against the
   whole previous interval `[u,v]`.

On the other side, results that guarantee only one large prime factor in every
short interval are still much weaker than what this route needs. For example:

- Merikoski, *Large prime factors on short intervals* (2021):
  [arXiv:1805.05123](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05123)

This gives an integer with a very large prime factor in intervals of length
about `x^(1/2) log^1.39 x`, which is far from a right-core semiprime in every
interval of length `x^theta` with `theta < 1/2`.

So, from known tools, I do not see a route that upgrades the current
computational heuristic to a theorem strong enough for EP1212.

## 7. The Exact Strongest Gap

Within the present power-buffer route, the exact missing theorem is:

> **Uniform rough-semiprime slab-survival theorem.**
> For some `theta in (1/3,1/2)` and `H(x)=floor(x^theta)`, the right-core
> buffered digraph contains a forward-surviving component crossing every
> sufficiently large dyadic slab.

Equivalently, one needs a theorem that gives, on every large slab, enough
right-core semiprime sources `(u,v)` with enough right-core semiprime
successors `w` satisfying the two-window avoidance conditions, plus enough
dependency control to prevent all mass from collapsing into finitely many dead
branches.

The gap is therefore **not** just "find semiprimes in `(x, x+x^theta]`". It is:

```text
rough semiprime successor counts
+ moving forbidden-factor exclusions from [u,v]
+ regenerative clearance with respect to v
+ slab-by-slab survival / dependency control.
```

No part of the current notes proves that theorem.
