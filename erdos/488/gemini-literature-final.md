## QUESTION 2: Sieve theory analog of the ancestor lemma

I need the following result, or something close to it:

Let B = {b_1, ..., b_s} be a set of primes (or prime powers). Define
L_B(y) = |{n ≤ y : b ∤ n for all b ∈ B}| (divisibility avoidance count).

Now let B' = B \ {3} (remove 3 from the sieve set).

I need to compare the "oscillation" of L_B vs L_{B'} at rescaled evaluation points. Specifically, if L_B has a "bad ratio" (L_B(t)/L_B(s) is large relative to t/s), does L_{B'} evaluated at rescaled points (s' ≈ (h/3)s, t' ≈ (h/3)t for some h ≥ 5) always have enough "slack" to compensate?

This feels like it should be related to:
- The Buchstab identity (relates sieving with primes up to z to sieving with primes up to a smaller w)
- The fundamental lemma of sieve theory (bounds on sieve remainders)
- Selberg's sieve (comparison of sieve levels)
- Jacobsthal's function g(n) (largest gap between integers coprime to n)

Specifically: is there a known result that says "removing a prime p from the sieve set B and evaluating at a scale factor p/q larger always produces a count that dominates the original sieve's oscillation"?

The closest statement I can formalize:
If B ⊇ {2,3} and L_B(s) = 1 (only the integer 1 survives up to s), then for B' = B \ {3} and s' = ⌈(h/3)(s+1)⌉ - 1, t' = ⌊(h/3)t⌋ with h ≥ 5:

  2m·L_{B'}(s') - n·L_{B'}(t') ≥ n·L_B(t) - 2m·L_B(s)

where n = (s+1)a - 1, m = ta for the child, and a is the child's modulus.

Is this, or anything similar, in the sieve theory literature?
