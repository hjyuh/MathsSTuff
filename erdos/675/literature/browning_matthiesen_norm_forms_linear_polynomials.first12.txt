in Q, let X be the variety deﬁned by the equation NK(x) = P (t). Combining additive
combinatorics with descent we show that the Brauer–Manin obstru ction is the only ob-
struction to the Hasse principle and weak approximation on any smoo th and projective
model of X.

3. Technical tools
3.1. Geometry of numbers. We will need to be able to estimate the number of lattice
points in shifts of suﬃciently well-behaved expanding regions. Let n ∈ Z>0 and let B be
any bounded subset of Rn. Write T B = {T x : x ∈ B} for the dilation by T > 0. The
following result is classical.
Lemma 3.1. Assume that B is bounded and that for any ε ∈ (0, 1) the ε-neighbourhood
of the boundary ∂B has volume O(ε). Let a ∈ Rn and let T ⩾ 1. Then
# (Zn ∩ (T B + a)) = vol( B)Tn +O(Tn− 1).
Proof. Observe that the ε-neighbourhood of ∂T B arises as dilation by T of the εT − 1-
neighbourhood of ∂B and has volume O(εTn− 1). The lemma follows (cf. [18, App. A])
since
# (Zn ∩ (T B + a)) = vol (( Zn ∩ (T B + a)) + [0, 1)n),
and the set in the latter volume agrees with T B + a outside an O(1)-neighbourhood of
∂T B + a. □
It is not hard to see that any non-empty bounded set in Rn whose boundary is ( n − 1)-
Lipschitz parametrisable satisﬁes the hypotheses of Lemma 3.1. Th is follows, for example,
from the proof of Lemma 2 in [25, §6]. Similarly, bounded convex sets in Rn satisfy the
hypotheses of the lemma (see [18, Cor. A.2], for example).
Given a ﬁnite set of ﬁxed regions B1,..., Bm to which Lemma 3.1 applies, the hypotheses
of the lemma are also met by any set which arises through unions and in tersections of these
sets. In particular it applies to intersections of bounded convex se ts with bounded sets
having (n − 1)-Lipschitz parametrisable boundary.
