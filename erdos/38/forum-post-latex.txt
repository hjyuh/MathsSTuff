\textbf{Convention.} I require $0 \in A$, which is standard in the Schnirelmann density literature (Erd\H{o}s [Er36c], Mann [Ma42], Nathanson). The element $0$ does not affect $\sigma(A) = \inf_{N \geq 1} |A \cap \{1,\ldots,N\}|/N$ but participates in shifts. Without $0 \in A$, the problem is trivially false at small $N$ for any $B$ with $\min(B) \geq 2$: take $A = \{1,3,5,\ldots\}$ and $N = 2$.

\textbf{Theorem.} $B = 3\mathbb{N}+2 = \{2, 5, 8, 11, \ldots\}$ is not an additive basis of any finite order, yet for every $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}_0$ with $0 \in A$ and $\sigma(A) = \alpha \in (0,1)$, and every $N \geq 1$, there exists $b \in B$ such that
$$|(A \cup (A+b)) \cap \{1,\ldots,N\}| \geq \left(\alpha + \frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{15}\right)N.$$

\textbf{Non-basis property.} For any $h \geq 1$: $hB \subseteq \{n : n \equiv 2h \pmod{3}\}$, a single residue class. So $B$ is not a basis of any finite order.

\textbf{Proof outline.} Write $x_i = \mathbf{1}[i \in A \cap [0,N]]$, $\delta = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i$, $G_b = \sum_{i=1}^N x_{i-b}(1-x_i)$, $g = \max_{b \in B \cap [1,N]} G_b/N$, and $d_b = \sum_{i=1}^N |x_i - x_{i-b}|$.

\textbf{Step 1 (GCD Propagation).} Since $2, 5 \in B$ and $\gcd(2,5) = 1$, the identity $5 - 2\cdot 2 = 1$ gives the decomposition $x_i - x_{i-1} = (x_i - x_{i-5}) + (x_{i-5} - x_{i-3}) + (x_{i-3} - x_{i-1})$. Summing the triangle inequality and bounding boundary terms explicitly: $d_1 \leq d_5 + 2d_2 + 4 \leq 6gN + 10$.

\textbf{Step 2 (Halved Lipschitz).} The extended sequence $(0, 1, x_1, \ldots, x_N, 0)$ starts and ends at $0$, so up-transitions equal down-transitions: $T^+ = T^- = V/2 \leq (d_1+2)/2$. Since $G_{k+1} - G_k$ sums over a subset of these transitions: $|G_{k+1} - G_k| \leq (d_1 + 2)/2 \leq 3gN + 6$. Every $k \in [1,N]$ is within distance 1 of $B$ (max gap 3), so $G_k \leq 4gN + 6$.

\textbf{Step 3 (Average Gain).} Writing $S = \sum_{k=1}^N G_k$ as a count of pairs $(m,n)$ with $m \in A$, $n \notin A$, $m < n$, and using the Schnirelmann bound to force gap positions $c_j \geq j/(1-\alpha)$: $S \geq \alpha(1-\delta)^2 N^2 / (2(1-\alpha))$.

\textbf{Step 4 (Upper Bound).} Using $|B_N| \geq (N-1)/3$ and the Step 2 bound: $S \leq 3gN^2 + 2gN + 4N + 4$.

\textbf{Step 5 (Combining).} Define $h_N(\delta) = \delta + \alpha(1-\delta)^2 N^2 / (2(1-\alpha)(3N^2+2N)) - (4N+4)/(3N^2+2N)$. Then $\delta + g \geq h_N(\delta)$. Since $h_N'(\delta) \geq 1 - \alpha/3 > 0$ on $[\alpha, 1)$, the minimum is at $\delta = \alpha$: $h_N(\alpha) = \alpha + [\alpha(1-\alpha)N^2/2 - 4N - 4]/(3N^2 + 2N)$. This exceeds $\alpha + \alpha(1-\alpha)/15$ when $(9p/2)N^2 - (2p+60)N - 60 \geq 0$ (where $p = \alpha(1-\alpha)$), whose positive root gives the threshold $N_0$.

\textbf{Step 6 (Discrete Argument).} For $N \geq 2$, take $b = 2 \in B$. If $G_2 \geq 1$: density gain $\geq 1/N \geq p/15$ when $N \leq 15/p$. If $G_2 = 0$: $x_m = 1 \Rightarrow x_{m+2} = 1$; since $x_0 = 1$ all evens lie in $A$, and since $\alpha > 0$ forces $x_1 = 1$ all odds lie in $A$, giving $\delta = 1$. For $N = 1$: density $= 1 \geq \alpha + f(\alpha)$.

\textbf{Step 7 (Overlap).} Discrete covers $N \leq \lfloor 15/p \rfloor$. Continuous covers $N \geq \lceil N_0(p) \rceil$. We verify $15/p > N_0(p)$ for all $p \in (0, 1/4]$: at worst case $p = 1/4$, RHS $= 123.2 < 135$. No gap. $\square$
