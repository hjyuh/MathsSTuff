Theorem: $B = 3\mathbb{N}+2 = \{2, 5, 8, 11, \ldots\}$ is not an additive basis of any finite order, yet for every $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}_0$ with $0 \in A$ and $\sigma(A) = \alpha \in (0,1)$, and every $N \geq 1$, there exists $b \in B$ such that $|(A \cup (A+b)) \cap \{1,\ldots,N\}| \geq (\alpha + \alpha(1-\alpha)/15)N$.

Non-basis: $hB \subseteq \{n : n \equiv 2h \pmod{3}\}$, one residue class per order.

Proof outline (7 steps):

Step 1 (GCD Propagation): Since $2, 5 \in B$ and $\gcd(2,5) = 1$, decompose $x_i - x_{i-1} = (x_i - x_{i-5}) + (x_{i-5} - x_{i-3}) + (x_{i-3} - x_{i-1})$. Summing the triangle inequality with explicit boundary terms: $d_1 \leq d_5 + 2d_2 + 4 \leq 6gN + 10$.

Step 2 (Halved Lipschitz): The extended sequence $(0,1,x_1,\ldots,x_N,0)$ starts and ends at $0$, so up-transitions equal down-transitions. This gives $|G_{k+1}-G_k| \leq (d_1+2)/2 \leq 3gN+6$. Since every $k$ is within distance $1$ of $B$ (max gap $3$): $G_k \leq 4gN+6$.

Step 3 (Average Gain): $S = \sum G_k \geq \alpha(1-\delta)^2 N^2/(2(1-\alpha))$ via the Schnirelmann bound forcing gap positions $c_j \geq j/(1-\alpha)$.

Step 4 (Upper Bound): $S \leq 3gN^2 + 2gN + 4N + 4$ using $|B_N| \geq (N-1)/3$.

Step 5 (Combining): The function $h_N(\delta) = \delta + g(\delta)$ is increasing on $[\alpha,1)$, so min at $\delta = \alpha$ gives $f \geq \alpha(1-\alpha)/6 - O(1/N)$, holding for $N \geq N_0$ from $(9p/2)N^2 - (2p+60)N - 60 \geq 0$.
